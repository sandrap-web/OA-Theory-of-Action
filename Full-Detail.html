Full functional separation with dedicated roles for Career Navigators, Trainers, Business Engagement, Mission Leaders and Data Specialists. No one is stretched across competing functions.

47% adopted★ Mission Leader
No team members identified for the OA Fellows program.
Interest has been expressed but no one has participated yet.
At least one team member has completed or is currently enrolled in OA Fellows.
At least one team member has completed or is enrolled. Fellows serve as internal champions guiding OA adoption.
Two or more team members have completed or are enrolled. Fellows actively mentor staff and serve as OA ambassadors across the network.
4. Referral Network & Technology
25% adopted★ Data Specialist
No referral technology in use. Referrals happen informally or not at all.
Limited or pilot use of referral technology. Some manual referral processes exist.
The organization has a referral process and partnerships with community organizations that provide support services.
Referral partnerships are formalized. The organization has a documented process for connecting people to community support services.
Automated Referral Technology (like UniteUs or FindHelp) is fully integrated. Electronic referrals are tracked from start to finish, ensuring no one falls through the cracks. Warm hand-off partnerships are guided by regional needs data.
5. Employer Partnerships
48% adopted★ Business Engagement
No employer engagement. No formal relationships with hiring partners.
Some employer contacts exist but no formal agreements or structured partnerships.
Formal referral partnerships are in place with local employers who offer job opportunities.
Employer partnerships are formalized with local, sector-driven employers. Relationships are based on real job placement activity.
Partnerships are driven by labor market data and include co-created training programs. Employers help design curricula and provide direct hiring pipelines for jobs that pay a living wage.
6. Evaluating Impact & Metrics
88% adopted★ Data Specialist
No data tracked. No system for measuring outcomes or program effectiveness.
Some data fields are tracked but inconsistently. Reporting is sporadic.
Basic data is tracked — enrollment, completion, placement, wages — and submitted regularly. A case management system is in use.
A defined set of individual-level data is collected using a case management system. Post-placement outcomes are tracked at 30, 60, 90 and 180 days. Data is submitted to GII monthly.
Comprehensive data is collected with extended follow-up through 365 days. The organization uses advanced tracking including credential attainment, wage growth, employment type and retention. Data drives continuous improvement.

7. Outreach & Recruitment
77% adopted★ Bus. & Comm. Engagement
No proactive outreach. People find services only through walk-ins or word of mouth.
Informal outreach or limited referrals from community contacts.
The organization uses community partner networks to reach potential participants. Outreach is intentional but not technology-driven.
Outreach uses established community partner networks. The organization actively recruits through diverse channels.
Outreach is technology-enabled using automated referral platforms and established referral partners to systematically reach people who need services most.
8. Registration & Intake
90% adopted★ Career Navigator
Intake is not aligned with key factors. No standardized process for gathering participant information.
Intake partially aligns with key factors. Some information is collected but the process is inconsistent.
Intake is aligned with basic key factors — demographic, work history and education information is captured for all participants.
Intake is aligned with the core set of OA data requirements. The process captures the information needed to determine eligibility and inform service planning.
Intake is comprehensive — capturing an extensive list of factors with thorough data capture. Every piece of information gathered feeds directly into personalized service design.
9. Needs Assessment
64% adopted★ Career Navigator
Not used. Participant barriers across life domains are not systematically evaluated.
Used inconsistently. Some participants receive a needs assessment, but it's not standard practice.
All participants complete a needs assessment covering housing, transportation, health, childcare, legal issues and more.
100% completion. Results directly inform support service referrals and service planning.
100% completion. Assessment results are integrated with referral technology to automatically connect participants to the right community resources.
10. Basic Education Assessment
21% adopted★ Career Navigator
Not used. Reading, math and English proficiency are not assessed.
Used inconsistently. Some participants are assessed, but there's no clear protocol for who should be.
Assessments are completed for participants without a diploma who are enrolling in occupational training.
Required for all participants without a diploma who enroll in occupational training. A valid assessment tool is used.
Same requirement. Assessment may be provided directly or through a referral partner. Results inform whether participants need adult basic education or GED preparation before training.
11. Career Readiness Assessment
46% adopted★ Career Navigator
Not used. Career interests and readiness gaps are not formally identified.
A tool has been identified but it's used inconsistently. Not all participants are assessed.
All participants complete a valid career readiness or interest assessment to help match them with career paths.
100% completion using a valid and reliable assessment tool.
A standardized tool (such as JOFI or O*NET Interest Profiler) is used for all participants to identify interests, preferences and career motivators.
12. Digital Skills Assessment
28% adopted★ Career Navigator
Not used. Digital literacy is not measured.
A tool has been identified but it's used inconsistently.
All participants complete a valid digital skills assessment to determine their comfort and gaps with technology.
A valid digital skills assessment is completed by all participants.
All participants complete the Northstar Digital Literacy assessment — a nationally recognized standard — to measure proficiency with computers, internet, email and workplace technology.
13. Assessment Selection
56% adopted★ Program Manager
No formal assessments are required for enrollment. Staff decide on an ad hoc basis.
Some participants complete assessments, but there's no standard for which ones or when.
All participants complete at least one valid assessment. Career Navigators select based on individual needs.
100% of participants complete at least one valid assessment selected by their Career Navigator based on individual circumstances.
100% complete the Northstar digital assessment plus additional assessments selected by their Career Navigator based on individual needs.
14. Orientation
70% adopted★ Program Coordinator
Not consistently offered. Participants may start services without understanding what's available.
Offered but not completed by all participants. Attendance is optional or not tracked.
All participants complete an orientation session before enrollment that explains services, sets expectations and introduces the program.
100% completion before enrollment. Can be group or individual, virtual or in-person.
100% completion. Establishes clear understanding of the full service menu, roles, responsibilities and what participants can expect throughout their journey.
15. Individual Career & Financial Plan
58% adopted★ Career Navigator
Not consistently offered. Participants don't have a written plan guiding their services.
Some plans are created but they lack specific, measurable goals. Plans may not be updated.
All participants complete a personalized career and financial plan that documents goals and next steps.
100% complete a plan with SMART goals — Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound. Covers both career pathway and financial stability.
Plans also include progress tracking, financial stability goals (budgeting, savings, credit) and are treated as living documents that evolve as participants progress.
📚 Deliver the Right Services — Equip & Connect 7 service areas
16. Wraparound Support Services
92% adopted★ Career Navigator
No support services to remove life barriers. Participants are on their own for housing, transportation, childcare, etc.
Limited referrals to community resources. Coverage is inconsistent and not coordinated.
Support services are aligned to individual needs — help with housing, transportation, healthcare, childcare and food security.
Comprehensive support is coordinated through community partners. Services matched to each person's documented needs. Can be provided directly or through referral.
Full support network coordinated through referral technology. Every referral is tracked to completion. The organization ensures participants actually connect with services — not just get a phone number.
17. Financial Education
44% adopted★ Trainer
Not offered. No guidance on managing money, building credit, or financial planning.
Offered occasionally but attendance isn't tracked. Content is informal.
Financial education is offered with a structured curriculum. Attendance is documented.
All participants are offered financial education covering budgeting, banking, credit, savings and benefits enrollment. Delivery can be coaching, workshops, or online.
Comprehensive program with pre/post assessments. Covers budgeting, debt management, tax preparation, benefits navigation and planning for benefit cliffs.
18. Foundational Skills Training
68% adopted★ Trainer
Not offered. No structured training on workplace skills like communication, teamwork, or professionalism.
Offered inconsistently with limited tracking. Content varies.
Training is offered with a documented curriculum and completion data is tracked.
Training is offered and informed by employer feedback — teaching the workplace skills local employers say matter most.
Includes at least one course from the Skills to Succeed Academy — a proven curriculum that builds employability through real-world scenarios. Aligned with employer needs.
19. Digital Skills Training
32% adopted★ Trainer
Not offered. No training on computer use, software, or online tools.
Some digital skills training is offered but not aligned to a structured framework.
Training follows the Goodwill Digital Career Accelerator (GDCA) framework — from basic computer use to advanced certifications.
Follows GDCA framework, tailored to employer demand and individual needs. In-person, virtual, or through partners.
Full GDCA implementation aligned to local labor market demand. Participants can progress from basic digital literacy to industry-recognized certifications.
20. Occupation-Specific Training
51% adopted★ Trainer
Not offered. No technical or vocational training programs.
Some programs aligned to demand but credential tracking is limited.
Training is aligned with local labor market demand. Participants can earn industry-recognized credentials.
Occupational training is aligned with employer input and local demand. Programs lead to credentials that employers trust.
Training is co-created with employer partners and focused on living-wage occupations. Employers help design curricula and provide direct hiring pipelines.
21. Career Exploration
37% adopted★ Program Coordinator
Not offered. No hands-on opportunities to learn about different careers.
Occasional activities like job fairs or tours, but limited and not consistently tracked.
Hands-on career exploration is consistently offered — job shadowing, site tours, informational interviews, or VR simulations.
Career exploration activities are offered and aligned with in-demand occupations in the local market.
Full range of experiences — internships, pre-apprenticeships, apprenticeships, on-the-job training and learn-and-earn programs — all connected to living-wage careers.
22. Job Search & Prep
92% adopted★ Career Navigator
Not offered. No help with resumes, interviews, or applications.
Some help is available on an ad hoc basis, but it's not structured.
Resume and interview preparation are offered as standard services.
Comprehensive support including resume creation, mock interviews, application help and professional presentation guidance.
Complete job readiness support — mock interviews, soft skills coaching, LinkedIn profile help, online application navigation and strategic guidance on disclosing barriers like criminal records or employment gaps.
🚀 Launch Careers, Stay Connected — Launch & Evaluate 3 service areas
23. Job Placement
84% adopted★ Business Engagement
Not offered. No structured help connecting people to employers.
Limited placement support. Some referrals happen but the process is informal.
Job placement assistance is offered with documented processes.
The organization actively engages employers and helps people secure jobs. Placement processes are formalized and outcomes are tracked.
People are placed in jobs that meet or exceed the local living wage threshold (per MIT Living Wage Calculator). Employer partnerships provide direct hiring pipelines.
24. Retention Services
69% adopted★ Career Navigator
No structured follow-up after someone gets a job.
Follow-up happens at an irregular cadence. Some people are checked on, others aren't.
Follow-up is becoming structured. The organization checks in with placed participants at regular intervals.
Structured follow-up at 30, 60, 90 and 180 days. Staff identify problems early — transportation, workplace conflicts, childcare — and connect people to support before issues become job loss.
Follow-up extends to 365 days. Staff provide crisis intervention, workplace coaching, benefits navigation and financial stability support throughout the full first year. Employer communication protocols allow early intervention.
25. Alumni Engagement
12% adopted★ Program Coordinator
No alumni engagement. Once people complete the program, the connection ends.
Informal engagement — occasional events or check-ins, but nothing structured.
At least one formal method of alumni engagement is being developed or piloted.
Not required at Gold level, but encouraged. Organizations that engage alumni report stronger outcomes and higher participant motivation.
At least one formal alumni method established — networking, mentorship, advisory roles, continuing education, or career advancement support. Alumni serve as ambassadors and mentors.
