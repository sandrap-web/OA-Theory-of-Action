Goodwill Opportunity Accelerator® (OA) — Career Navigator Role Guide DRAFT

Frontline Staff · Career Navigators
What You Own, What You Support and What Belongs to Someone Else
May 2026 Goodwill Industries International

Your Role at a Glance

What You Do
You work directly with participants every day — helping them understand where they are, build a plan, overcome barriers, find jobs and stay employed. You are the person they rely on most throughout their entire journey. From the moment someone walks through the door to a year after they've started working, you are their primary point of contact and their strongest advocate.
Your Workload
You touch 19 of 25 service areas — more than any other role. That's why what you do matters so much and why your professional development is a priority.
★ 9 areas you OWN ◆ 6 areas you SUPPORT ○ 4 areas you're INFORMED on
Why Certification Matters
With 9 primary areas — the heaviest load of any role in the OA model — deep competence isn't optional. That's why Career Navigator certification through the Goodwill Career Coach and Navigator Certificate Program is a core OA requirement. The volume and complexity of your work demands professional training. Certification doesn't just check a box — it gives you the tools to do this job well and sustain it over time.
How to use this guide: This guide shows only the service areas that involve your role. For each area, you'll see what your responsibility looks like at each adoption stage — from Prospective to Exemplary. Use this to understand exactly what's expected of you today and what it takes to move to the next level.

★ Your Primary Areas — You Own These 9 areas

These are the areas where you are the person directly responsible. If these don't happen well, it's on you. Here's exactly what each one looks like at every stage.

8. Registration & Intake
90% adopted★ You Own This
You're not conducting a standardized intake. There's no consistent process for what you ask or capture when someone walks in.
You're collecting some information during intake, but the process isn't standardized. What you capture varies depending on the day or the person.
You conduct intake for every participant, capturing key demographic, work history and education information. You have a consistent process you follow each time.
You conduct a structured intake aligned with OA data requirements for every participant. You capture the information needed to determine eligibility, identify barriers and begin planning their path. Your intake data feeds directly into their personalized plan.
Your intake is comprehensive — you capture an extensive set of information that gives you a complete picture of each person's starting point. Every piece of data you collect is intentional and feeds directly into personalized service design and long-term outcome tracking.
9. Needs Assessment
64% adopted★ You Own This
You're not conducting needs assessments. You may be aware of some barriers but aren't systematically evaluating them.
You conduct needs assessments for some participants but not all. It depends on time, caseload, or how obvious the barriers are.
You conduct a needs assessment for every participant — evaluating barriers across housing, transportation, health, childcare, legal issues, food security and more. This is how you understand the full picture.
100% of your participants complete a needs assessment. You use the results to connect people to the right support services and to inform every service decision you make.
100% completion. Your assessment results are integrated with referral technology so you can automatically connect participants to community resources — and track whether they actually received help.
10. Basic Education Assessment
21% adopted★ You Own This
You're not assessing reading, math, or English proficiency. Participants may enter training without knowing if they're academically ready.
You assess some participants but there's no clear protocol. You may not know which participants should be assessed.
You assess all participants without a diploma who are enrolling in occupational training. You use a valid tool to measure reading, math and English skills.
Same as C — required for all non-diploma participants entering training. You use results to determine whether someone needs adult basic education or GED prep before occupational training.
Same requirement. You may refer participants to a partner for the assessment itself, but you're responsible for ensuring it happens and using results to guide the right service path.
11. Career Readiness Assessment
46% adopted★ You Own This
You're not formally assessing career interests or readiness. You may ask informally, but there's no structured tool.
You have a tool but you're not using it consistently. Some participants get assessed, others don't.
Every participant you work with completes a valid career readiness or interest assessment. You use results to help match them with realistic career paths.
100% of your participants complete this assessment. You use a valid, reliable tool and the results directly inform the career pathway documented in their plan.
You use a standardized tool like JOFI or O*NET Interest Profiler for every participant. Results identify specific work-related interests, preferences and motivators that shape their entire career path.
12. Digital Skills Assessment
28% adopted★ You Own This
You're not measuring digital literacy. You may assume participants can use technology — or assume they can't — without actually knowing.
You've identified a digital skills tool but you're not using it consistently.
Every participant completes a digital skills assessment so you know exactly where they stand with technology — from basic computer use to workplace software.
100% completion using a valid assessment. You use results to determine whether someone needs digital skills training and at what level.
Every participant completes the Northstar Digital Literacy assessment. You use results to place participants in the right level of digital training and track their progress over time.
15. Individual Career & Financial Plan
58% adopted★ You Own This
You're not creating written plans with participants. Services happen without a clear roadmap.
You create plans for some participants, but they lack specific goals. Plans may sit in a file and never get updated.
Every participant you work with has a personalized career and financial plan. It documents their goals and the steps to get there.
100% of your participants have a plan with SMART goals — specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and time-bound. The plan covers both career pathway and financial stability. You reference it in every interaction and update it as circumstances change.
Plans are living documents. They include career goals, financial stability goals (budgeting, savings, credit), progress tracking and are updated regularly. Every service you recommend connects back to what's documented in this plan.
16. Wraparound Support Services
92% adopted★ You Own This
You're not connecting participants to support services. If someone has a housing crisis or can't afford childcare, they're on their own.
You make some referrals to community resources, but it's inconsistent. You may not follow up to see if participants actually connected with help.
You actively identify barriers for each participant and connect them to support — housing, transportation, healthcare, childcare, food security, legal aid. Support is aligned to what each person actually needs.
You coordinate comprehensive support through community partners. For every participant, you match services to their documented needs. You follow up to make sure connections are made — not just phone numbers handed out.
You coordinate support through referral technology that tracks every referral from start to finish. You can see whether participants actually received services. No one falls through the cracks on your watch.
22. Job Search & Prep
92% adopted★ You Own This
You're not helping participants with job search. There's no resume help, interview coaching, or application support.
You help some participants with resumes or interviews on an ad hoc basis, but it's not a standard part of your work.
You provide resume and interview preparation as a standard service for your participants.
You provide comprehensive job search support — resume creation, mock interviews, application coaching and professional presentation guidance. You help each participant target positions aligned with their career plan and skills.
You provide complete job readiness support — including mock interviews, soft skills coaching, LinkedIn profiles, online application navigation and strategic coaching on how to address barriers like criminal records or employment gaps. You prepare participants to present their best selves.
24. Retention Services
69% adopted★ You Own This
Once someone gets a job, your involvement ends. There's no structured follow-up.
You check in with some placed participants, but it's irregular. There's no schedule or system.
You're beginning to follow up with participants after placement at regular intervals to catch problems early.
You conduct structured follow-up at 30, 60, 90 and 180 days after placement. You're the person who catches problems early — a car breaking down, a conflict with a supervisor, a childcare disruption — and connects people to support before it becomes job loss.
You follow up for a full year — at 30, 60, 90, 180 and 365 days. You provide crisis intervention, workplace coaching, benefits navigation and financial stability support. You work with employers (with participant consent) to intervene early when issues emerge. You are the safety net.
◆ Your Contributor Areas — You Actively Support These 6 areas

Someone else leads these areas, but you play an important supporting role. Here's what that looks like for you.

2. Career Navigator Certification
32% adopted◆ You SupportLed by: Program Manager
Your role: You are the one getting certified. Complete the Goodwill Career Coach and Navigator Certificate Program. At Gold, at least 50% of Career Navigators are certified within the first year. At Platinum, 100%. This certification directly builds the skills you need for the 9 areas you own — it's an investment in your ability to do this work well.
7. Outreach & Recruitment
77% adopted◆ You SupportLed by: Bus. & Comm. Engagement
Your role: You support outreach by helping identify the right people to serve and by being available when referrals come in. You're often the first person a new participant meets after outreach brings them through the door. Your welcoming presence sets the tone for everything that follows.
13. Assessment Selection
56% adopted◆ You SupportLed by: Program Manager
Your role: While the Program Manager oversees assessment standards, you are the one selecting which assessments each participant takes based on their individual needs. Your professional judgment determines what each person needs — that's why your expertise matters so much here.
14. Orientation
70% adopted◆ You SupportLed by: Program Coordinator
Your role: You support orientation by being available to meet participants, answer questions and begin building the relationship that will carry through their entire journey. You may co-facilitate sessions. This is often where participants first connect with you — make it count.
17. Financial Education
44% adopted◆ You SupportLed by: Trainer
Your role: You reinforce financial concepts in your one-on-one work with participants. You connect financial education to their career plan — helping them understand how career goals and financial goals work together. If someone's career plan targets $18/hour, you help them think about what that means for their budget, savings and stability.
23. Job Placement
84% adopted◆ You SupportLed by: Business Engagement
Your role: You prepare participants for placement and ensure they're ready. You communicate participant skills, preferences and barriers to the Business Engagement team so placements match career plans. You're the bridge between the person and the job opportunity — no one knows their story better than you.
○ Areas Where You Stay Informed 4 areas

You don't lead or actively support these, but you should know what's happening because it affects your participants and your work.

1. Functionally Aligned Staffing
77% adopted○ InformedLed by: Mission Leader
Why it matters to you: How roles are structured determines your caseload, your focus and whether you can specialize in what you do best. When staffing is functionally aligned, you can focus on career navigation instead of being pulled into business engagement, data entry, or training delivery.
3. OA Fellows Program
47% adopted○ InformedLed by: Mission Leader
Why it matters to you: OA Fellows bring back knowledge, strategies and tools that improve how you serve participants. Knowing who the Fellows are in your organization helps you access their expertise when you need it.
18. Foundational Skills Training
68% adopted○ InformedLed by: Trainer
Why it matters to you: You refer participants to foundational skills training — things like communication, teamwork, time management and professionalism. Knowing what's covered helps you make better referrals and reinforce concepts in your one-on-one work.
21. Career Exploration
37% adopted○ InformedLed by: Program Coordinator
Why it matters to you: When participants explore careers through job shadowing, tours, or apprenticeships, it directly informs the plans you build together. Staying connected to what exploration options are available helps you guide better career decisions.
