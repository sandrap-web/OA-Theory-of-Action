How to read this page: This logic model shows how the Opportunity Accelerator works at two connected levels. Level 2 (Local) is the primary, long-term logic model that describes how member organizations serve participants and is the model intended for external sharing (e.g., funders, partners). Level 1 (Network) is the enablement layer — it shows the supporting work GII does to build organizational capacity across the Goodwill network. In Level 1, outcomes are organized as short-, medium-, and long-term to reflect how GII’s support drives progressive change across the Goodwill network. Read each level left to right: Inputs → Activities → Outputs → Outcomes.
Acronym Key: GII = Goodwill Industries International · OA = Opportunity Accelerator · CN = Career Navigator · TA = Technical Assistance · ToA = Theory of Action · ART = Accountability Reporting Tool · MOLÉ = Mission Optimization Learning Event · ICFP = Individualized Career and Financial Plan · P/C/I = Primary / Contributing / Informed (role framework)
🌐 Level 1: Network (GII) — Enablement Layer What GII does to build organizational capacity
GII Inputs
  • OA shared practice model (25 service areas)
  • Funding (GII and external sources)
  • Experienced internal staff and program design capacity
  • Evaluation and learning partnerships
  • CN certification (free to members)
  • OA Fellows program
  • TA and consultation capacity (GII mission team)
  • Fidelity tools & adoption criteria
  • MOLÉ & peer learning events
  • OA training materials (one-pagers, GW Academy modules)
GII Activities
  • Provide stage-differentiated TA and OA consultations
  • Deliver free CN certification
  • Facilitate the OA Fellows program
  • Convene peer learning (MOLÉ, webinars, advisory councils)
  • Develop and disseminate fidelity and self-assessment tools
Outputs
  • # organizations completing self-assessment
  • # organizations receiving technical assistance and OA consultations
  • # Career Navigators trained and certified
  • # local team members participating in OA Fellows program
  • # peer learning sessions delivered
  • # organizations assigning staff to core OA functions
  • # organizations implementing standardized intake, recruitment, and data processes
  • # organizations adopting OA fidelity self-assessment tools
  • # organizations achieving OA implementation milestones
Outcomes
Short-Term Outcomes
  • % organizations demonstrating increased understanding of OA core components following GII support
  • % organizations beginning to adopt standardized OA processes across service areas
  • % organizations actively using OA tools and resources provided
Medium-Term Outcomes
  • % organizations implementing OA core functions consistently across service delivery areas
  • % organizations demonstrating alignment with standardized program design and delivery
  • % organizations using data to guide program improvement and decision-making
Long-Term Outcomes
  • % organizations sustaining OA implementation over time
  • % organizations maintaining fidelity to OA standards across core functions
  • % organizations demonstrating continuous improvement using performance data
GII’s network-level work produces organizations that are clear, consistent, and equipped ▶ which enables strong local implementation in Level 2
🏢 Level 2: Local (Member Organizations) — Primary Logic Model What organizations do on the ground to serve participants
Organization Inputs
  • Committed Mission Leader
  • Aligned staffing (8 functional roles)
  • Certified Career Navigators (CNs)
  • Employer partnerships
  • Community connections
  • Data systems and ART (Accountability Reporting Tool)
  • Funding and facilities
Organization Activities
  • Outreach and recruitment
  • Registration and intake
  • Administer valid assessments
  • Co-create ICFPs with participants
  • Coordinate wraparound supports
  • Deliver skills training (foundational, digital, occupation-specific)
  • Career exploration and job search preparation
  • Support participants in securing employment
  • Retention follow-up (30, 90, 180, 365 days)
  • Alumni engagement
Outputs
  • # individuals reached through multichannel outreach (community partnerships; on-site, digital, and mass-media marketing)
  • # individuals completing intake (annually)
  • # individuals assessed with valid instruments (within 30 days of intake)
  • # individuals with an Individualized Career and Financial Plan (ICFP) developed
  • # individuals referred to wraparound services
  • # individuals completing training programs (annually)
  • # individuals entering employment (annually)
  • # individuals with retention follow-up at 30, 90, 180, and 365 days
  • # individuals engaged as alumni (within 12 months post-exit; at least one alumni touchpoint)
Outcomes
Short-Term
  • Services matched to assessed needs
  • Clear plan with goals and action steps
  • Barriers identified
Intermediate
  • Barriers addressed
  • Job-relevant skills built
  • Employment secured (see definition below)
Long-Term
  • Employment retained and advanced
  • Quality employment achieved (see definition below)
  • Financial literacy and stability growing
  • Ongoing connection through the alumni network

📖 Key Definitions

  • Reached through outreach: documented contact (in-person, phone, email, or digital engagement) with a prospective participant.
  • Employment secured: any employment entered during or after program participation, regardless of Goodwill’s brokering role.
  • Quality employment: employment meeting defined criteria including a living wage, access to benefits (e.g., health insurance, paid leave), and career advancement potential.
  • Engaged as alumni: participation in at least one alumni touchpoint (event, mentorship, survey, or service) within 12 months of program exit.

★ Long-Term Impact

Enhanced employment outcomes Career advancement & economic mobility Financial stability Strengthened member organizational capacity (expanded offerings, deepened service intensity, stronger local partnerships) Network cohesion Contribution to community economic strength (indirect, system-level)

📋 Assumptions (Testable)

  1. Organizations progressing through the five maturity stages will demonstrate measurable differences in OA practice fidelity scores.
  2. Organizations with clearly defined role assignments (P/C/I framework) will demonstrate higher OA fidelity scores than those without.
  3. Mission Leaders presented with a clear structural roadmap will invest in organizational preparation activities at higher rates than those without one.
  4. Organizations exposed to a structured progressive roadmap will advance through OA stages more quickly than those without.
  5. Organizations with access to sufficient funding, staffing, and infrastructure will progress through OA stages at higher rates.
  6. GII’s differentiated TA model will produce measurable improvements in OA implementation across organizations at different maturity stages.
  7. Participants who engage with the full continuum of OA services will demonstrate stronger employment, retention, and advancement outcomes than those who engage partially.
  8. Stakeholders engaged in validation activities (e.g., feedback sessions, advisory councils, MDRC partnership) will produce more reliable and usable framework refinements than those developed without stakeholder input.

⚠️ External Factors

  • Labor market conditions (regional variation)
  • Funding environment (federal, state, private)
  • Policy & regulatory landscape
  • Community demographics & population needs
  • Economic conditions (recession, inflation, housing)
