Meet People Where They Are
Phase 1 — Assess & Plan
  • No consistent intake or assessments
  • No standard way to understand needs, skills or goals
  • Basic intake exists but not standardized
  • Some assessments used, not for everyone
  • Orientation may be offered; attendance not tracked
  • Key information collected for every person
  • At least one validated assessment completed
  • Orientation introduces services and sets expectations
  • Structured intake captures work history, education and life circumstances
  • Assessments cover career interests, skills and digital abilities
  • Personalized career and financial plan with SMART goals
  • Comprehensive intake provides complete picture of each person
  • Full assessment set including Northstar Digital Literacy
  • Plans include career goals, financial stability and progress tracking

Phase 2 — Equip & Connect
  • Few or no structured services

Launch Careers, Stay Connected
Phase 3 — Launch & Evaluate
  • No structured job placement help
  • No follow-up; outcomes not tracked
  • Some placement help; follow-up inconsistent
  • Basic outcomes may be gathered, not systematically
  • Documented placement process
  • Regular check-ins after placement beginning
  • Basic outcomes tracked
  • Organization connects people to employers
  • Follow-up at 30, 60, 90 and 180 days
  • Key outcomes tracked monthly, submitted to GII
  • Living-wage placements through employer pipelines
  • Follow-up for a full year (30–365 days)
  • Formal alumni program; outcomes drive improvement

Important: Most organizations will be at different stages for different rows — and that's completely normal. For example, an organization might be strong in job placement but still developing its assessment process. Each of the 25 service areas that make up OA moves at its own pace. This overview shows the big picture. The Full Detail View breaks each of these four areas down into all 25 specific service areas with step-by-step progression descriptions.

How GII Supports You at Every Stage

GII provides three tiers of support matched to where your organization is in the adoption journey.
Stages A–B

Self-Directed

OA tools, readiness assessment and learning events to build awareness and explore fit.

Stages B–C

Group Advisory

2-month consultation with peer cohort, action plan and guided implementation support.

Stages C–E

Individualized

4–6 month mission consultation with dedicated advisor, fidelity monitoring and continuous improvement.

Who Owns What — The 8 Roles That Make OA Work

Every one of the 25 service areas has a named owner — someone who is directly responsible for making it happen. Here are the eight roles and what they do. Smaller organizations may have one person covering multiple roles; what matters is that every area has someone accountable.
Mission Leader
★ 2 primary areas
Makes the big decisions — staffing structure and OA strategy
Program Manager
★ 2 primary areas
Keeps everything running — oversees operations across all 25 service areas
Career Navigator
★ 9 primary areas
Works directly with people every day — the most hands-on role and the heaviest workload
Business Engagement
★ 3 primary areas
Builds relationships with employers to create job opportunities and training partnerships
Community Engagement
★ 1 primary area
Connects with community organizations for outreach, referrals and support services
Program Coordinator
★ 3 primary areas
Manages orientation sessions, career exploration activities and alumni programs
Trainer
★ 4 primary areas
Teaches job skills, digital skills, financial literacy and workplace readiness
Data Specialist
★ 2 primary areas
Tracks what's working — manages outcome data, reporting and referral technology
